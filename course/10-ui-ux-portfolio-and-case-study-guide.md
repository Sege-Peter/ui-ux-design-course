# Module 10: UX Case Study Architecture & Portfolio Guide

Design hiring managers do not hire you based on pretty Dribbble mockups alone. They hire you for your **problem-solving process, strategic thinking, user empathy, and communication skills**.

---

## 1. Quality Over Quantity

- **The Golden Rule:** Two to three deep, end-to-end case studies will win you a top design role faster than ten superficial visual galleries.
- **Scannability:** Design leads spend **less than 3 minutes** reviewing your portfolio. Your case study must be scannable with clear headings, bulleted takeaways, before/after comparisons, and annotated visual proof.

---

## 2. The 8-Part Case Study Anatomy

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ 1. Project Title, Hero Visual & One-Sentence Value Hook   │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. Context: Role, Timeline, Team Size, Platforms, Tools  │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. The Core Problem & Business Impact Goals              │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 4. Research Discovery & Key Pivot Insights               │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 5. Information Architecture & Early Wireframe Iterations │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 6. High-Fidelity Solution & Interactive Prototype        │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 7. Usability Testing Feedback & Iterative Changes        │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 8. Measurable Impact, Next Steps & Key Retrospective     │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Breakdown of Key Sections:

#### 1. The Context Block
- **My Role:** E.g., Lead Product Designer (User Research, Wireframing, Design System, Testing).
- **Duration:** E.g., 6 weeks (Sprint 1 to Sprint 3).
- **Tools:** Figma, FigJam, Google Stitch, Maze.
- **Collaborators:** 1 Product Manager, 2 Frontend Engineers, 1 UX Researcher.

#### 2. The Core Problem & Impact Goals
- State clearly what was failing in the existing experience.
- Link UX goals directly to business metrics: *"Checkout drop-off rate was 68%, costing an estimated $42,000 in lost monthly recurring revenue."*

#### 3. The Research "Pivot" Insight
Highlight the moment when research changed your initial assumptions:
> *"We assumed users wanted more advanced search filters. However, during 5 contextual user interviews, we discovered users were overwhelmed by existing filters and simply wanted a smart search bar that understood natural language queries."*

#### 4. Showing the Messy Middle
Show sketches, discarded concepts, and paper wireframes. Explain *why* you eliminated Concept A in favor of Concept B. This demonstrates mature design reasoning.

#### 5. Measurable Outcomes & Retrospective
- Quantify results whenever possible: *"Redesigned flow reduced average checkout time from 3m 45s to 1m 15s and increased checkout conversion by 24%."*
- State what you learned: *"If I had more time, I would have conducted accessibility audits with screen reader users earlier in the sprint."*

---

## 3. Preparing for the Design Portfolio Interview

When presenting your work during an on-site or virtual design interview:

1. **Don't read your case study word-for-word:** Treat the presentation like a narrative story with a protagonist (the user), an antagonist (the friction/problem), and a climax (the validated solution).
2. **Highlight team collaboration:** Emphasize how you compromised with engineers when technical constraints arose.
3. **Be ready to answer:**
   - *"Why did you choose this layout over an accordion?"*
   - *"How did you validate that users understood this icon?"*
   - *"If you could redesign one thing today, what would it be?"*
