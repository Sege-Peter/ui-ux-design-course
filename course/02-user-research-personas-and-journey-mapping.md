# Module 2: User Research, Personas & Journey Mapping

Designing without research is like embarking on a road trip without a map. User research uncovers genuine needs, cognitive biases, mental models, and pain points before writing a single line of code or pushing pixels.

---

## 1. Qualitative vs. Quantitative Research

| Dimension | Qualitative Research ("Why" & "How") | Quantitative Research ("How Many" & "What") |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Data Type** | Non-numerical observations, verbal quotes, emotional reactions | Numerical metrics, statistical trends, ratios, percentages |
| **Methods** | 1-on-1 User Interviews, Contextual Inquiry, Diary Studies | Surveys, Google Analytics, Product Heatmaps, Funnel Analysis |
| **Sample Size** | Small (typically 5 to 12 targeted participants) | Large (hundreds or thousands of data points) |
| **When to Use** | Early discovery to understand user motivation and root causes | Later evaluation to measure conversion, drop-off, or feature usage |

---

## 2. Conducting Effective User Interviews

The goal is to hear about real past behaviors, not hypothetical promises.

### The Golden Rules of Interviewing:
- **Ask open-ended questions:** Never ask "Do you like this feature?" (leads to false compliments). Instead ask: *"Can you walk me through the last time you completed this task?"*
- **Embrace silence:** Give the participant 3-5 seconds after they finish speaking; they will often fill the silence with deeper reflections.
- **The "5 Whys" technique:** Dig past surface answers to reach the underlying emotional root problem.

---

## 3. Creating Actionable User Personas

A persona is an archetype of your core user base based on real research data—not fictional stereotypes.

### Anatomy of an Effective Persona:
1. **Name & Archetype:** E.g., *"Sarah Jenkins – The Busy Logistics Coordinator"*.
2. **Demographics & Context:** Age, occupation, technical proficiency, device usage environment (e.g., noisy warehouse on an Android tablet).
3. **Core Goals:** What are they trying to accomplish? (e.g., *"Dispatch 50 delivery trucks within 30 minutes with zero scheduling conflicts"*).
4. **Pain Points & Frustrations:** What is slowing them down? (e.g., *"Current system requires switching between 3 disconnected tabs to verify driver availability"*).
5. **Key Quote:** *"I don't need fancy animations; I need fast, accurate data entry."*

---

## 4. User Journey Mapping

A **Customer Journey Map** visualizes the chronological timeline of steps a person takes to achieve a goal, mapping their emotions, touchpoints, and friction points.

```text
Phases:       [ 1. Discovery ] ──> [ 2. Onboarding ] ──> [ 3. Execution ] ──> [ 4. Completion ]
User Goal:      Find tool           Create account        Configure job          Download report
Touchpoint:     Landing page        Sign-up modal         Dashboard editor       Email receipt
Emotions:       😄 Curious          😐 Anxious            😫 Overwhelmed         😊 Relieved
Friction:       Vague pricing       Too many fields       No auto-save           Delayed email
Opportunity:    Transparent tiers   Social OAuth login    Real-time saving       Instant in-app link
```

---

## 5. Framing Problems with "How Might We" (HMW) Statements

HMW statements turn user frustrations into generative opportunities for brainstorming:

- **Pain Point:** *"Users abandon checkout because they don't know the final shipping fee until the last step."*
- **HMW Statement:** *"How might we give shoppers transparent, real-time shipping estimates on the product page itself?"*

## 🎯 Module 2 Practical Exercise

1. Interview 2 friends or colleagues about their experience using an online grocery delivery or shopping app.
2. Synthesize the findings into an empathy map: **Says**, **Thinks**, **Does**, **Feels**.
3. Draft a 1-page User Persona summarizing their primary goals and top 3 friction points.
